From VLM-Generated Evidence to Observable Properties: A Traceable Taxonomy for Surveillance Video Analysis


Interactive supplementary material

Abstract

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can describe actions and scene context to support anomaly analysis in surveillance videos. However, their heterogeneous descriptions do not provide a consistent, traceable set of properties for comparing evidence across scenarios. We address the task of transforming free-form VLM-generated surveillance evidence into a corpus-grounded taxonomy of observable properties. Our human-reviewed method combines semantic embeddings and density-based clustering with independent cluster-suitability assessment and curated organization into semantic families, while preserving links to the original statements, generating models, and source videos.

Applied to evidence generated from UCF-Crime and CamNuvem, the method produced 222 candidate clusters. Two reviewers agreed on 94.59% of suitability decisions (κ = 0.78). Following reconciliation, 194 clusters were retained and organized into 12 semantic families covering actions, interactions, objects, and scene conditions described by VLMs for videos with anomalous and non-anomalous dataset labels. The resulting taxonomy provides an inspectable foundation for comparing surveillance evidence and deriving application-specific properties.

Keywords: Vision-Language Models, Video Understanding, Surveillance Video, Observable Properties, Semantic Evidence, Taxonomy, Video Anomaly Analysis.
